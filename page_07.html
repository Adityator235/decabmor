D. Overview of Current Business Strategy Relating to Customer Loyalty and Repeat Engagement

StarCare's current approach to customer loyalty is entirely informal. There is no formal loyalty program, no prescription refill reminder system, no structured way to track patient retention, and no system for measuring patient satisfaction beyond online reviews. Their current strategy relies on word-of-mouth and personal relationships — which works to a point, but it's not scalable or measurable.

Current Strengths

  • 4.5-star Google rating from 160+ reviews — patients consistently praise the staff's personalized attention
  • Free home delivery — a retention tool that builds habit and dependence
  • Custom compounding — a differentiator that chains cannot replicate
  • Welcoming store environment — teal walls, warm wood floors, open counter layout that encourages interaction

Current Gaps

  • No loyalty program — patients have no structured reason to return beyond habit or convenience
  • No refill reminders — no system to notify patients when it's time to refill
  • No patient retention tracking — no way to know if a patient has stopped coming
  • 2.6-star Yelp rating — billing complaints are driving patients away silently
  • No competitive response — no strategy to counter chain pharmacy loyalty programs

Competitive Landscape

Prosper's pharmacy market is pretty saturated with national chains. StarCare isn't just competing with one chain — they're competing with all of them:

THE CHAIN PARADOX Here's the thing: chains score the lowest in JD Power's 2025 study — 643/1,000 for chain drug stores, the worst of any pharmacy type. They trail supermarkets by 72 points and mass merchandisers by 63 points. Meanwhile, chain drug store closures are creating opportunities for other pharmacy types. But chains still dominate in convenience: 24/7 hours, drive-thrus, apps. StarCare's advantage isn't convenience — it's connection. The strategy needs to amplify what chains literally cannot replicate: personal relationships.
