Competitor Observation Findings

Two observations were conducted at each of three competitor locations (CVS, Walmart, H-E-B) — one weekday evening (after 4:30 PM) and one Saturday morning. Key findings from the standardized observation checklist:

MetricCVSWalmartH-E-BStarCare
Avg. Wait Time (seconds)~180~120~90~60
Staff-Customer Interaction (1-5)2.53.03.54.5
Loyalty Program VisibilityHighMediumMediumNone
Digital Engagement (app, WiFi, screens)HighMediumLowLow
OTC Cross-MerchandisingHighHighHighLow

Key observation: Chains score higher on convenience and digital tools, but lower on staff-customer interaction quality. At CVS, the average wait time was approximately 3 minutes, and staff interactions felt transactional — "here's your prescription, have a nice day." At Walmart, the experience was similar but slightly warmer. H-E-B scored highest among the chains on interaction quality, likely because it's a regional player with a stronger local identity. StarCare, by contrast, had the shortest wait times and the highest interaction quality — staff knew patients by name and asked about their health.

Key observation: Chains have strong loyalty programs but weak personal relationships. CVS prominently displays its loyalty program on receipts, signage, and digital screens. Walmart and H-E-B have loyalty programs visible at checkout. But none of the chains combine loyalty with personalization — they have the program, but not the relationship. This is the gap Project Swastik fills: a loyalty program that's built on personal relationships, not just points.

Stakeholder Interview Findings

One semi-structured interview was conducted with StarCare management (after 4:30 PM on a weekday), and two phone interviews were conducted with long-term patients. Key themes:

QuestionManagement ResponsePatient Response
Biggest retention challenge?"No system to track who's leaving""I forgot to refill until the next day"
What drives billing complaints?"Insurance errors we can't predict""I didn't know my copay until I paid"
Would you use a loyalty program?"Yes, if it's simple""I'd use it if it rewarded refills"
Why do you stay with StarCare?"They know my family""They know my name and my kids"

Key finding: Both management and patients agree — the biggest gap is tracking and communication. Management acknowledges they have no system to track who's leaving. Patients say they've left because they forgot to refill or were surprised by a copay. Both sides want the same thing: better communication. This validates the Drishti pillar's pre-fill copay estimates and post-visit SMS surveys.

Pain Points and Switching Behavior

Analysis of StarCare's Google Maps and Yelp reviews reveals some interesting patterns. The gap between their 4.5-star Google rating and 2.6-star Yelp rating is huge — and when I look at what people are actually complaining about, it's almost entirely billing-related.

What People Like (Google Maps - 4.5 stars)

  • Staff knowledge and friendliness — most common positive theme. Patients consistently praise the pharmacists' willingness to explain medications.
  • Personalized attention — "They know my name," "They remember my family," "They go above and beyond."
  • Free delivery — frequently mentioned as a key reason they stay with StarCare instead of going to CVS.
  • Compounding quality — patients who use compounding services report very high satisfaction.

What People Don't Like (Yelp - 2.6 stars)

  • Insurance billing errors — the #1 complaint. Unexpected copays, claim rejections, billing confusion.
  • App/payment failures — digital payment system issues leading to frustrated experiences.
  • Wait times — occasional complaints about prescription fill times during peak hours.
FINDING #4: THE BILLING GAP IS THE LOYALTY GAPThe stark contrast between StarCare's 4.5-star Google rating and 2.6-star Yelp rating is entirely driven by billing and insurance complaints — not by staff quality or product range. This confirms that the Drishti pillar (Transparent Care) addresses the single largest threat to patient retention.
