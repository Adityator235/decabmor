# Case Study & Presentation Tips — Winning Strategies

## Understanding the Task

### Performance Indicators (PIs) Drive Your Grade
The case study will list 5-6 Performance Indicators. These are **where the bulk of your points come from**. Every recommendation should tie back to at least one PI.

### Analyze the Scenario
1. **Identify your role** — you are hired consultants
2. **Identify the evaluator's position** — the judge is the business owner/manager
3. **Identify the core challenge** — what problem needs solving?
4. **Note key figures** — budget limits, timelines, constraints
5. **Map each PI to your recommendations** — ensure full coverage

### Role-Play Setup
- **You** = hired consultants presenting a strategic plan
- **Judge** = business owner/manager
- Presentation begins immediately after introductions by adult assistant
- Explain the proposed strategic plan at the start — let you complete without interruption unless judge asks a question
- Each participant **must take part**

**The Strategic Plan section is worth 36% of your written entry score.** Spend the most time here.

### Recommended Page Allocation (20-page max)

| Section | Pages | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|
| Title Page | Not counted | Business name, your name, event, date, school |
| Table of Contents | Not counted | With page numbers, can be 1+ pages |
| **I. Executive Summary** | 1-3 | Write LAST — highlight problem, solution, key financials |
| **II. Introduction** | ~1 | Business, target market, current loyalty strategy |
| **III. Research Methods** | ~1.5 | Primary + secondary research design |
| **IV. Findings & Conclusions** | ~4-5 | Data analysis, charts, key insights, SWOT |
| **V. Proposed Strategic Plan** | ~6-8 | **Core of your entry** — detailed, PI-aligned |
| **VI. Proposed Budget** | ~2-3 | Budget breakdown, ROI, breakeven |
| **VII. Bibliography** | Not counted | Required — list all sources |
| **VIII. Appendix** | Numbered | Optional — survey instruments, raw data, extra charts |

### Page Numbering Rules
- Title page and TOC: **not numbered**
- First page of body (Executive Summary): numbered **"1"**
- Page numbers continue through bibliography and appendix
- Maximum **20 numbered pages** including appendix

## Presentation Structure (15 Minutes)

### Official Rules
- Presentation begins immediately after introductions
- Explain the strategic plan first — complete without interruption
- Judge may then ask questions based on notes or written entry
- Hand-carried visual aids only (no wheeled carts, moving straps, generators)
- **No electrical power or internet** — you furnish your own materials
- Setup time **counts toward** your 15 minutes
- Materials like flyers, brochures, pamphlets, business cards may be handed to or left with judges
- Sound may be used at conversational volume

### Recommended Breakdown
| Segment | Time | Content |
|---------|------|---------|
| **Opening/Hook** | 1 min | Grab attention — surprising stat, question, or story |
| **Business & Problem** | 2 min | Context — who is the business, what's the challenge |
| **Research & Findings** | 3 min | Key data points that drive your recommendations |
| **Recommendations** | 5 min | Your strategic plan — tie to PIs |
| **Financials & ROI** | 2 min | Budget, projected returns, implementation feasibility |
| **Closing** | 1 min | Memorable summary, call to action |
| **Q&A Buffer** | 1 min | Built in for judge questions |

### Visual Aids
- **Create a skeleton outline** in advance — one slide per PI
- Use **professional charts and graphs** — no cluttered slides
- **Minimal text** on slides — you tell the story, slides support
- Include **supplementary materials**: agendas, budgets, timelines
- **No electrical power or internet** — prepare for no-projection presentation
- Setup time **counts toward** your 15 minutes — practice quick setup

## Presentation Delivery Tips

### Do's
- ✅ **Dress professionally** — first impressions matter
- ✅ **Make eye contact** — engage with the judge, not your slides
- ✅ **Vary your tone** — keep it engaging, not monotone
- ✅ **Use pauses** — let key points sink in
- ✅ **Be confident** — judges rely on your certainty to trust your recommendations
- ✅ **Address each PI explicitly** — judges check these off
- ✅ **Bring handouts** — budget sheets, survey summaries
- ✅ **Stay in character** — you are consultants, judge is the owner

### Don'ts
- ❌ **Don't read from slides** — you know this material
- ❌ **Don't go over 15 minutes** — practice with a timer
- ❌ **Don't propose generic solutions** — tie everything to the specific business
- ❌ **Don't skip the financials** — judges want to see ROI
- ❌ **Don't be defensive in Q&A** — acknowledge gaps, show you've thought about alternatives

## Anticipating Q&A

### Common Judge Questions
1. **"Why did you choose this approach over alternatives?"**
   - Prepare 2-3 alternatives you considered and why you rejected them

2. **"How will you measure success?"**
   - Have specific KPIs ready: retention rate, CLV, NPS, repeat purchase rate

3. **"What's your break-even point?"**
   - Know your breakeven calculations cold

4. **"What if the business doesn't have budget for this?"**
   - Have a phased/lean alternative ready

5. **"How does this connect to buying and merchandising?"**
   - Always tie back to merchandise decisions, OTB, assortment

6. **"What are the risks?"**
   - Show you've thought about implementation challenges

7. **"How does your research support this recommendation?"**
   - Reference specific survey results or data points

## 🏆 Sample 1st Place Entry — "Turn the P.A.G.E." Campaign

Reference: [1st Place BMOR Sample Entry](10-Resources/SAMPLE-ENTRY-1-EXTRACT.txt)

### Business Context
- **Bookends**, Ridgewood, NJ — community bookstore est. 1984
- 25,000-30,000 visitors annually to downtown Ridgewood
- Hosts author and celebrity events, draws from NYC metro area
- Previous topic: CSR (structure applies to any topic)

### Research Methods Used (8 different methods)
| Method | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| Owner interview (Walter Boyer) | Deep business understanding |
| Current & former employee interviews | Operational gaps |
| Barnes & Noble employee interview | Competitor perspective |
| Community survey | Customer sentiment (100+ respondents) |
| Focus group | Qualitative depth |
| Secret shopper analysis | Objective experience assessment |
| Competitor observation | Benchmarking against rivals |
| Competitor case study reviews | Learn from competitor strategies |

### Key Findings (Data-Driven with Specific Numbers)
| Finding | Data Point |
|---------|------------|
| Used books are growing | 70%+ students prefer used; market grew 5.5% in 1 year |
| Event communication is weak | Third-party reviews cite lack of transparency for last-minute changes |
| Community values social causes | 52.1% value sustainability, 66.4% value mental health, 44% value D&I |
| Employee engagement lags | Former employee noted Barnes & Noble offers better wages/benefits |

### SWOT Analysis (Grounded in Research)
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|------------|
| Small, welcoming, cherished by community | Outdated website and communication channels |
| Author/celebrity events attract regional visitors | Lack of transparency for last-minute event changes |
| Strong charitable giving, fair employee treatment | Employee engagement could improve |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---------------|---------|
| Secondhand book market projected to grow $45B in 10 years | Barnes & Noble nearby — well-funded competitor |
| Locals interested in sustainability, mental health, D&I | Shift to online shopping (no online presence) |

### Strategic Plan: Turn the P.A.G.E. Campaign

| Letter | Initiative | Description |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **P** | **Pre-Owned Book Marketplace** | App for customers to resell books (ISBN + photo → AI pricing); online merchandise sales |
| **A** | **App-Based Transparency** | Event notifications, push alerts for last-minute changes, stakeholder inbox |
| **G** | **Giving & Awareness** | "Awareness month" book displays, round-up donations at checkout (6-month campaign) |
| **E** | **Employee Engagement** | Extended benefits, volunteer options, task rotation, weekly bonus reward system |

### KPIs (Specific, Measurable, with Measurement Method)

| KPI | Target | How Measured |
|-----|--------|-------------|
| Third-Party Rating | Above 4.5 to 5.0 | Google Maps, TripAdvisor, Yelp feedback |
| App Adoption Rate | Above 60% | App Store feedback history |
| Donation Round-Up Participation Rate | 40% | Measured via transactions |
| Employee Retention Rate | 90% | Employee status records |
| Monthly Second-Hand Book Transactions | 150+ | Measured via the app |
| Customer & Employee Satisfaction Index | 85% | Internal satisfaction surveys |

### Budget Format (Detailed with Percentages)

| Activity | Associated Costs | % of Total | Subtotal |
|----------|-----------------|------------|----------|
| Pre-Owned Book Marketplace & App-Based Transparency | App development, payment fees, App Store publication | 77.3% | $4,422 |
| Giving & Awareness Initiatives | New shelving for "awareness month" displays | 12.2% | $695 |
| Employee Engagement | $50 reward gift card to chosen employee weekly | 10.5% | $600 |
| **TOTAL** | | **100%** | **$5,717** |

### ROI Calculation

```
Projected Profit:              $30,060
Total Costs:                   $5,717
ROI:                           ($30,060 - $5,717) / $5,717 × 100 = 425.80%
Breakeven Point:               April 2027 (Q2)
Amount Donated (social impact): $2,620
```

### Timeline Format (12-Month Quarterly)

| Quarter | Activities |
|---------|------------|
| **Q1** (Jan-Mar) | App coding/development, beta testing |
| **Q2** (Apr-Jun) | App rollout, beta testing stages |
| **Q3** (Jul-Sep) | Build "Awareness Month" displays, round-up donations, staff reflection |
| **Q4** (Oct-Dec) | Staff meetings, employee bonus rollout, employee volunteering |

## Preparation Timeline

### 8-12 Weeks Before Competition
- [ ] Select partner business (local, accessible for primary research)
- [ ] Interview store management about challenges
- [ ] Develop survey tools and get approval
- [ ] Begin secondary research (industry trends, competitors)

### 4-7 Weeks Before Competition
- [ ] Distribute surveys (target 50-100+ respondents)
- [ ] Conduct in-depth interviews (owner, employees, former employees, competitors)
- [ ] Conduct focus group and secret shopper analysis
- [ ] Analyze data — identify patterns and insights
- [ ] Draft the 20-page report
- [ ] Calculate budgets and ROI projections
- [ ] Develop strategic plan with memorable framework

### 1-3 Weeks Before Competition
- [ ] Finalize written entry — check penalty point checklist
- [ ] Get all signatures on required forms
- [ ] Design visual aids — practice with no-projection scenario
- [ ] Conduct 3+ mock presentations with different judges
- [ ] Practice Q&A — anticipate tough questions
- [ ] Time each presentation segment
- [ ] Print supplementary materials for presentation day

## Penalty Point Checklist

Avoid these penalty triggers:
- [ ] Exceeding 20-page limit (excluding title page, TOC)
- [ ] Not including Statement of Assurances
- [ ] Unsigned academic integrity form
- [ ] Missing required sections (I through VIII)
- [ ] Generic proposal not specific to the business
- [ ] No primary research included
- [ ] Not focused on buying/merchandising (general marketing instead)
- [ ] Page numbering errors (title/TOC should NOT be numbered)
- [ ] Missing bibliography (required)
- [ ] Executive summary exceeds 3 pages

## Winning Differentiators

### What Separates Top Teams from the Rest

1. **Deep primary research** — 8+ methods (owner, employees, former employees, competitors, surveys, focus groups, secret shoppers, observations)
2. **Creative framework** — memorable acronym or campaign name (e.g., "Turn the P.A.G.E.")
3. **Specific, measurable KPIs** — each with a target AND a measurement method
4. **Detailed budget** — with percentages, subtotals, and clear cost breakdown
5. **ROI with breakeven** — calculated, with projected profit and timeline
6. **SWOT analysis** — grounded in primary research data
7. **Quarterly timeline** — specific activities per phase
8. **Clear buying/merchandising connection** — loyalty drives inventory decisions
9. **Professional visual aids** — charts, graphs, program mockups
10. **Confident, professional delivery** — practiced, polished, passionate, in character
