IV. Findings & Conclusions (continued)

Finding 4: Loyalty Programs Must Go Beyond Discounts

Current loyalty programs in Prosper's pharmacy market are transactional: CVS's ExtraBucks gives cash back, Walmart's Price Match guarantees low prices, Costco's Price Match does the same. None create emotional loyalty. None build relationships. None differentiate based on care quality.

Research shows that patients value health-focused loyalty benefits far more than pure discounts. The top desired loyalty benefits in pharmacy are:

Desired Benefit Importance Rating Current Market Offering
Free health screenings (blood pressure, A1C, cholesterol) 4.6 / 5 Only Walmart (limited BP checks)
Personalized medication reminders 4.5 / 5 Chain apps only (generic)
Free delivery 4.4 / 5 StarCare, CVS
Seasonal wellness offers (flu shots, vitamins) 4.3 / 5 None
Points redeemable for OTC products 4.1 / 5 CVS ExtraBucks (closest match)
Priority pharmacist consultation 4.0 / 5 None
Direct discount on OTC products 3.8 / 5 Walmart, Costco

Key insight: Health-focused benefits rank above pure discounts. Patients want a pharmacy that invests in their health, not just one that lowers prices. This is the strategic foundation for StarCare's loyalty program: a rewards system built on wellness participation, not just spending.

Finding 5: The Household Decision-Maker Is the Key Target

Prosper's pharmacy demand is driven by household decision-makers — primarily parents (45% of StarCare's projected patient mix) who manage prescriptions for children, spouses, and sometimes aging parents. This segment is the most valuable because:

  • Volume: They fill 8–12 prescriptions per year per household (vs. 1–3 for occasional shoppers)
  • Cross-category potential: They also purchase OTC products, vitamins, baby supplies, and veterinary medications
  • Amplification: They refer other families (school networks, community groups)
  • Retention: Once a household commits to a pharmacy, they rarely switch — switching costs are high (insurance paperwork, record transfer)

Implication: StarCare's loyalty program should be designed for the household, not the individual. A family that joins the program gets one account with profiles for all household members — each with their own medication list, health goals, and seasonal reminders. This mirrors how families actually use pharmacies.

KEY CONCLUSION: THE LOYALTY GAP The Prosper pharmacy market has a clear loyalty gap: chain programs are purely transactional (discounts, price matching), while patients want health-focused, personalized, community-connected loyalty. StarCare is positioned to fill this gap — but only if it acts before a chain pharmacy (H-E-B, Costco) expands its service model. The window is 12–18 months.
