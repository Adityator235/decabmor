I. Executive Summary

StarCare Pharmacy (StarcareRx) is an independent community specialty and compounding pharmacy at 210 S. Preston Rd, Suite B, Prosper, TX 75078, serving Prosper and surrounding Collin County. Prosper is one of the fastest-growing and most affluent cities in the United States - a town of 45,600+ residents with a median household income of $195,281, the highest in Texas. StarCare is a family-owned pharmacy with a 4.5-star Google rating from 160+ reviews, free home delivery, and a philosophy of "personal care, like family." Yet despite strong reviews, StarCare faces a critical challenge: no formal loyalty program exists, billing and insurance confusion drives away customers who cannot afford to lose a single patient household, and major chains (CVS, Walmart, H-E-B, Costco) are actively poaching StarCare's customer base.

THE OPPORTUNITY Chain drug stores scored just 643 out of 1,000 in the JD Power 2025 U.S. Pharmacy Study - the lowest of any pharmacy type, 72 points below supermarkets and 54 points below mail order. Chain drug store customers are significantly more likely to switch pharmacies in the next 12 months than any other segment. This is not a threat to StarCare - it is an opening. But StarCare must be ready to capture and retain those customers. Right now, it has no loyalty program, no structured retention strategy, and no system for turning one-time visitors into lifetime patients.

The Problem

Independent pharmacies lose 3-5% of their customer base annually. The average return rate at independent pharmacies is just 42% - meaning nearly 6 out of 10 patients who fill a prescription at StarCare once will not return. Meanwhile, 58% of customers will switch pharmacies if they find better convenience (Pharmacy Market Research, 2024), and only 23% of independent pharmacies have digital loyalty programs - even though 72% of customers want personalized pharmacy services.

StarCare's specific challenges are threefold: (1) No loyalty infrastructure - StarCare has no formal loyalty program, no prescription refill reminder system, and no structured way to track patient retention. (2) Billing and insurance transparency - despite a 4.5-star Google rating, StarCare's Yelp rating is 2.6/5, driven by complaints about insurance billing errors, unexpected copays, and app payment failures. (3) Chain pharmacy competition - CVS operates multiple locations in Prosper, Walmart and H-E-B have pharmacies in town, and Costco Pharmacy (643/1,000 JD Power score) is the second-highest-rated pharmacy type. Prosper's affluent, young families are highly price-sensitive despite high incomes - they will switch for convenience but will stay for trust.

The Solution: Project Swastik

Project Swastik (conducive to well-being in Sanskrit) is a three-pillar loyalty strategy designed specifically for independent pharmacy:

Pillar 1 - Drishti (Vision): Transparent Care. A billing transparency initiative that eliminates insurance confusion before it happens. Every patient receives a clear, written explanation of their copay before filling - not after. Automated prior authorization alerts 3-5 days before the due date. A post-visit satisfaction survey (via WhatsApp or SMS) catches billing issues within 24 hours. This addresses StarCare's #1 complaint driver: billing and insurance confusion.

Pillar 2 - Seva (Service): Personalized Wellness. A tiered loyalty program that rewards prescription refill consistency, OTC/wellness purchases, and referral behavior. Prescription refills earn stamps (hybrid stamp/points model). OTC and wellness purchases earn points redeemable for health screenings, vitamin bottles, or product discounts. Tiered benefits: Seed to Herb to Wellness to Swastik. Modeled on Apollo Pharmacy's CRM system, which achieved a 28% to 42% repeat purchase rate and 61% to 82% prescription refill compliance.

Pillar 3 - Milap (Connection): Community Connection. Community health events, local sponsorships, and relationship-building that turns StarCare from a pharmacy into a health partner. Free blood pressure screenings at local schools. Immunization clinics at Prosper ISD community events. Sponsorship of Prosper High School athletics and PISD academic programs. Personalized follow-up calls when a patient starts a new medication. This leverages StarCare's greatest competitive advantage over chains: personal relationships.

Pillar 1: Drishti - Transparent Care

Problem: Billing and insurance confusion is the #1 driver of patient churn at StarCare. The gap between the 4.5-star Google rating and 2.6-star Yelp rating is entirely billing-related.

Initiatives:

  1. Pre-fill copay estimates - Before every prescription is filled, the patient receives a clear written estimate of their copay. This eliminates surprise charges at the point of pickup.
  2. Prior authorization alerts - Automated alerts sent 3-5 days before the PA deadline, giving patients time to respond to their prescriber.
  3. Post-visit SMS surveys - Within 24 hours of prescription pickup, patients receive a 3-question SMS survey: copay clarity, wait time satisfaction, and overall rating. Negative responses trigger a follow-up call within 4 hours.
WHY THIS WORKSThe pre-fill copay estimate directly addresses the #1 Yelp complaint. The PA alert reduces fill delays. The post-visit survey catches issues before they become negative reviews. Together, these three initiatives transform StarCare's weakest area into a trust-building opportunity.

Pillar 2: Seva - Personalized Wellness

Problem: StarCare has no formal loyalty program. Patients have no structured reason to return beyond habit or convenience.

Initiatives:

  1. Hybrid stamp/points card - Prescription refills earn stamps (5 refills = 1 free compounding service or health screening). OTC/wellness purchases earn points (1 point per $1 spent).
  2. Tiered benefits - Four tiers: Seed (new patient), Herb (3+ refills), Wellness (6+ refills + 5 OTC purchases), Swastik (12+ refills + referral). Each tier unlocks additional benefits.
  3. Referral rewards - Existing patients who refer a new patient receive a free health screening or product discount. Leverages StarCare's strength in personal relationships.

Pillar 3: Milap - Community Connection

Problem: StarCare is perceived as a pharmacy, not a health partner. Patients fill prescriptions but don't see StarCare as part of their health ecosystem.

Initiatives:

  1. Free blood pressure screenings - Quarterly screenings at Prosper ISD schools (PTA meetings, back-to-school nights). Positions StarCare as a community health resource, not just a prescription filler.
  2. Immunization clinics - Partner with Prosper ISD for school-based flu shot clinics. Builds relationships with families at the point of need.
  3. Sponsorships - Prosper High School athletics and PISD academic programs. Logo on team jerseys, event banners, and school publications. Creates brand visibility in the community.
  4. Personalized follow-up calls - When a patient starts a new medication (chronic condition), a follow-up call within 7 days. "How are you feeling on this medication? Do you have any questions?" This is the kind of care chains literally cannot provide.
WHY THIS WORKSThese initiatives are impossible for chains to replicate at scale. A CVS cannot send a personal call to a patient who just started insulin. A Walgreens cannot sponsor Prosper High School with a genuine community connection. StarCare's small size is its competitive advantage here - it enables personal relationships that chains cannot match.

VIII. Appendix

Appendix A: Patient Survey Instrument

Section A: Demographics

A1. What is your age group? [Single choice: Under 18 / 18-24 / 25-34 / 35-44 / 45-54 / 55-64 / 65+]
A2. How many people are in your household? [Number: ___]
A3. How many prescriptions does your household fill per month? [Number: ___]

Section B: Pharmacy Usage

B1. How long have you been a StarCare patient? [Single choice: Less than 1 month / 1-6 months / 6-12 months / 1-2 years / 2+ years]
B2. How often do you fill prescriptions at StarCare? [Single choice: Every time / Most of the time / Sometimes / Rarely / Never (first-time)]
B3. Have you used StarCare's home delivery service? [Single choice: Yes, regularly / Yes, once or twice / No, but interested / No, not interested]
B4. How often do you purchase OTC or wellness products at StarCare? [Single choice: Every fill / Sometimes / Rarely / Never]
B5. Which StarCare services have you used? [Multiple choice: Prescription filling / Compounding / Immunizations / MTM / DME / Veterinary / Delivery / None]
B6. What was your reason for choosing StarCare? [Multiple choice: Personal recommendation / Proximity / Insurance requirement / Previous pharmacy closed / Staff recommendation / Other]

Section C: Satisfaction Drivers (5-point Likert scale)

C1. Staff knowledge about medications
C2. Wait time at pickup
C3. Clarity of copay explanation
C4. Store atmosphere and cleanliness
C5. Ease of online refill process
C6. Quality of compounding services
C7. Frequency of staff greeting by name
C8. Overall satisfaction with StarCare

Section D: Loyalty Interest

D1. Would you participate in a loyalty rewards program? [Single choice: Definitely / Probably / Maybe / Probably not / Definitely not]
D2. Which rewards would you value most? [Multiple choice: OTC discount / Free health screening / Priority service / Free delivery / Birthday gift / Referral bonus]
D3. How likely are you to refer a friend or family member to StarCare? [Single choice: Very likely / Somewhat likely / Neutral / Somewhat unlikely / Very unlikely]
D4. Would you prefer a stamp card or a points-based system? [Single choice: Stamp card / Points-based / No preference]
D5. How often would you check your loyalty program status? [Single choice: Every visit / Weekly / Monthly / Rarely]
D6. Would you like to receive health tips via SMS? [Single choice: Yes / No]
D7. What additional rewards would encourage you to return more often? [Open-ended]

Section E: Open Feedback

E1. What is one thing StarCare could do to improve your experience? [Open-ended]
E2. What do you appreciate most about StarCare? [Open-ended]
E3. Any other comments or suggestions? [Open-ended]

Appendix B: Competitor Observation Checklist

Each competitor location is visited 3 times (weekday morning, weekday afternoon, Saturday) over a two-week period. Observations recorded using the following checklist:

  1. Wait time (seconds from entry to staff interaction)
  2. Number of customers served per 15-minute interval
  3. Staff-customer interaction quality (1-5 scale)
  4. Loyalty program visibility (signage, receipts, staff mentions)
  5. OTC product placement and cross-merchandising
  6. Digital engagement (app promotion, WiFi branding, digital screens)
  7. Store cleanliness and organization
  8. Checkout upselling or recommendation behavior
  9. Overall customer experience rating (1-5)
