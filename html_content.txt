  <h1>I. Executive Summary</h1>
  <p>StarCare Pharmacy (StarcareRx) is a small independent pharmacy at 210 S. Preston Rd, Suite B in Prosper, Texas. Prosper is one of the fastest-growing cities in the country — over 45,000 people, a median household income of $195,281, which is the highest in Texas. StarCare has a 4.5-star Google rating from 160+ reviews, offers free home delivery, and their whole philosophy is "personal care, like family." But here's the problem: despite all that, they don't have a loyalty program. No system for keeping patients coming back. No way to track who's staying and who's leaving. And the big chains — CVS, Walmart, H-E-B — are actively trying to pull StarCare's customers away.</p>
  <div class="key-insight">
    <strong>THE OPPORTUNITY</strong>
    According to the JD Power 2025 U.S. Pharmacy Study, chain drug stores scored just 643 out of 1,000 in customer satisfaction — the lowest of any pharmacy type. Chain customers are the most likely to switch pharmacies in the next year. That sounds bad for chains, but it's actually an opportunity for StarCare. The question is: when those customers start looking for a new pharmacy, will StarCare have anything ready to keep them? Right now, the answer is no.

  <h2>The Problem</h2>
  <p>Independent pharmacies lose about 3-5% of their customers every year. The average return rate at independent pharmacies is just 42%, which means nearly 6 out of 10 patients who fill a prescription at StarCare once will never come back. Meanwhile, 58% of customers will switch pharmacies if they find something more convenient, and only 23% of independent pharmacies have any kind of digital loyalty program — even though 72% of customers want more personalized service from their pharmacy.</p>
  <p>StarCare's problems come down to three things. First, there's <strong>no loyalty infrastructure</strong> — no formal program, no refill reminders, no way to know if a patient is still coming back. Second, <strong>billing and insurance confusion</strong> is driving patients away. StarCare has a 4.5-star Google rating but only 2.6 stars on Yelp, and the difference is entirely about billing complaints — unexpected copays, insurance errors, payment app failures. Third, <strong>chain pharmacies are competing hard</strong>. CVS has multiple locations in Prosper, Walmart and H-E-B have pharmacies in town, and Costco Pharmacy (643/1,000 JD Power score) is the second-highest-rated pharmacy type. Prosper families are affluent but price-sensitive — they'll switch for convenience, but they'll stay for trust.</p>
  <h2>The Solution: Project Swastik</h2>
  <p>Project Swastik (which means "conducive to well-being" in Sanskrit) is a three-part loyalty strategy built specifically for an independent pharmacy like StarCare:</p>
  <p><strong>Pillar 1 — Drishti (Vision): Transparent Care.</strong> The biggest reason patients leave StarCare is billing confusion. This pillar eliminates that by giving every patient a clear written estimate of their copay before filling the prescription — not after. It sends automated alerts when a prior authorization is about to expire, and it follows up with patients via SMS or WhatsApp within 24 hours of pickup to make sure everything was clear. If a patient rates something negatively, a staff member calls them back within 4 hours. This turns StarCare's weakest point into a trust-building opportunity.</p>
  <p><strong>Pillar 2 — Seva (Service): Personalized Wellness.</strong> A tiered loyalty program that rewards patients for filling prescriptions consistently, buying OTC and wellness products, and referring friends. Prescription refills earn stamps — five refills gets you a free compounding service or health screening. OTC purchases earn points you can redeem for health screenings, vitamin bottles, or product discounts. The program has four tiers: Seed (new patient), Herb (3+ refills), Wellness (6+ refills + 5 OTC purchases), and Swastik (12+ refills + referral). Each tier unlocks more benefits. This is modeled on Apollo Pharmacy's CRM system, which took their repeat purchase rate from 28% to 42% and prescription refill compliance from 61% to 82%.</p>
  <p><strong>Pillar 3 — Milap (Connection): Community Connection.</strong> This is where StarCare has the biggest advantage over the chains. Free blood pressure screenings at local schools. Immunization clinics at Prosper ISD events. Sponsoring Prosper High School athletics. Personalized follow-up calls when a patient starts a new medication. Chains literally cannot do this at scale — a CVS pharmacist isn't going to call a patient who just started insulin to check on them. But StarCare can, because they're small enough to actually know their patients by name.</p>

  <h2>Pillar 1: Drishti - Transparent Care</h2>
  <p><strong>The Problem:</strong> Billing and insurance confusion is the #1 driver of patient churn at StarCare. The gap between the 4.5-star Google rating and 2.6-star Yelp rating is entirely billing-related. This is the single biggest leak in StarCare's loyalty bucket.</p>
  <p><strong>What We'll Do:</strong></p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Pre-fill copay estimates</strong> — Before every prescription is filled, the patient gets a clear written estimate of their copay. No surprises at pickup. This directly addresses the #1 Yelp complaint.</li>
    <li><strong>Prior authorization alerts</strong> — Automated alerts sent 3-5 days before the PA deadline, giving patients time to respond to their prescriber and avoid fill delays.</li>
    <li><strong>Post-visit SMS surveys</strong> — Within 24 hours of prescription pickup, patients receive a 3-question SMS survey: copay clarity, wait time satisfaction, and overall rating. Negative responses trigger a follow-up call within 4 hours.</li>
  </ol>
  <div class="key-insight"><strong>WHY THIS WORKS</strong>The pre-fill copay estimate directly addresses the #1 Yelp complaint. The PA alert reduces fill delays. The post-visit survey catches issues before they become negative reviews. Together, these three initiatives transform StarCare's weakest area into a trust-building opportunity.</div>
  <h2>Pillar 2: Seva - Personalized Wellness</h2>
  <p><strong>The Problem:</strong> StarCare has no formal loyalty program. Patients have no structured reason to return beyond habit or convenience. If a chain offers a discount nearby, they leave.</p>
  <p><strong>What We'll Do:</strong></p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Hybrid stamp/points card</strong> — Prescription refills earn stamps (5 refills = 1 free compounding service or health screening). OTC/wellness purchases earn points (1 point per $1 spent).</li>
    <li><strong>Tiered benefits</strong> — Four tiers: Seed (new patient), Herb (3+ refills), Wellness (6+ refills + 5 OTC purchases), Swastik (12+ refills + referral). Each tier unlocks additional benefits.</li>
    <li><strong>Referral rewards</strong> — Existing patients who refer a new patient receive a free health screening or product discount. This leverages StarCare's strength in personal relationships.</li>
  </ol>
