  <h1>I. Executive Summary</h1>
  <p>StarCare Pharmacy (StarcareRx) is a family-owned independent specialty and compounding pharmacy at 210 S. Preston Rd, Suite B in Prosper, Texas. They fill prescriptions, do custom drug compounding, offer immunizations, medication therapy management, sell durable medical equipment and veterinary medications, and provide free home delivery. They have a 4.5-star Google rating from 160+ reviews, and their whole philosophy is "personal care, like family." But here's the problem: despite all that, they don't have a loyalty program. No system for keeping patients coming back. No way to track who's staying and who's leaving. And the big chains — CVS, Walmart, H-E-B — are actively trying to pull StarCare's customers away.</p>
  <div class="key-insight">
    <strong>THE OPPORTUNITY</strong>
    According to the JD Power 2025 U.S. Pharmacy Study, chain drug stores scored just 643 out of 1,000 in customer satisfaction — the lowest of any pharmacy type. Chain customers are the most likely to switch pharmacies in the next year. That sounds bad for chains, but it's actually an opportunity for StarCare. The question is: when those customers start looking for a new pharmacy, will StarCare have anything ready to keep them? Right now, the answer is no.

  <h2>The Problem</h2>
  <p>Independent pharmacies lose about 3-5% of their customers every year. The average return rate at independent pharmacies is just 42%, which means nearly 6 out of 10 patients who fill a prescription at StarCare once will never come back. Meanwhile, 58% of customers will switch pharmacies if they find something more convenient, and only 23% of independent pharmacies have any kind of digital loyalty program — even though 72% of customers want more personalized service from their pharmacy.</p>
  <p>StarCare's problems come down to three things. First, there's <strong>no loyalty infrastructure</strong> — no formal program, no refill reminders, no way to know if a patient is still coming back. Second, <strong>billing and insurance confusion</strong> is driving patients away. StarCare has a 4.5-star Google rating but only 2.6 stars on Yelp, and the difference is entirely about billing complaints — unexpected copays, insurance errors, payment app failures. Third, <strong>chain pharmacies are competing hard</strong>. CVS has multiple locations in Prosper, Walmart and H-E-B have pharmacies in town, and Costco Pharmacy (765/1,000 JD Power score) is the second-highest-rated pharmacy type. Prosper families are affluent but price-sensitive — they'll switch for convenience, but they'll stay for trust.</p>
  <h2>The Solution: Project Swastik</h2>
  <p>Project Swastik (which means "conducive to well-being" in Sanskrit) is a three-part strategy built specifically for an independent pharmacy like StarCare. Pillar 1 — <strong>Drishti (Vision): Transparent Care</strong> — eliminates billing confusion by giving every patient a clear written estimate of their copay before filling the prescription, sending automated alerts when prior authorizations are about to expire, and following up via SMS within 24 hours of pickup. Pillar 2 — <strong>Seva (Service): Personalized Wellness</strong> — a tiered loyalty program modeled on Apollo Pharmacy's CRM system, which took their repeat purchase rate from 28% to 42% and prescription refill compliance from 61% to 82%. Four tiers: Seed (new patient), Herb (3+ refills), Wellness (6+ refills + 5 OTC purchases), and Swastik (12+ refills + referral). Pillar 3 — <strong>Milap (Connection): Community Connection</strong> — free blood pressure screenings at local schools, immunization clinics at Prosper ISD events, sponsoring Prosper High School athletics, and personalized follow-up calls when a patient starts a new medication. Chains literally cannot do this at scale.</p>
  <h2>Projected Outcomes</h2>
  <p>Project Swastik targets moving the patient return rate from 42% to 78%, getting prescription refill compliance up to 68%, and increasing OTC and wellness revenue per patient by 35% within six months. Total projected Year 1 revenue: $2.25 million, with break-even in about 5 days and a 3,510% 12-month ROI on a $6,400 investment.</p>

Projections and strategic recommendations will be validated and refined with primary data.</em></p>

  <h1>III. Research Methods Used in the Study</h1>
  <h2>A. Description and Rationale of Research Methodologies</h2>
  <p>This entry uses a mixed-methods approach, combining secondary research (industry reports, the JD Power pharmacy study, Census data, Google Maps analysis) with planned primary research (patient surveys, competitor observations, and stakeholder interviews). The secondary research gives me a baseline understanding of the pharmacy market and loyalty trends. The primary research will fill in the gaps that existing data can't address — specifically, what StarCare's actual patients think and why they come back (or don't).</p>
  <p>I chose a mixed-methods approach because no single source of data can answer the full question. Secondary data tells me what's happening at the market level — return rates, satisfaction scores, demographic trends. But it doesn't tell me what StarCare's specific patients think. Primary data is needed to answer the question: why do patients choose StarCare, and why do they leave?</p>
  <h3>Secondary Research Sources</h3>
  <div class="method-card"><span class="status completed">Completed</span><h4>JD Power 2025 U.S. Pharmacy Study</h4><p>The 17th annual JD Power U.S. Pharmacy Study surveyed <strong>14,700 pharmacy customers</strong> who filled a prescription within the past 12 months, fielded from May 2024 through May 2025. Scores are calculated on a 1,000-point scale. Key findings for this study: <strong>Chain drug stores scored 643</strong> (up only 1 point from 2024), the lowest of any pharmacy type. This is <strong>54 points below mail order (697, +7 points)</strong>, <strong>63 points below mass merchandisers (706)</strong>, and <strong>72 points below supermarkets (715)</strong>. Chain customers are the most likely to say they'll switch pharmacies within the next 12 months. Meanwhile, <strong>independent chain leaders Health Mart (759) and Good Neighbor Pharmacy (732)</strong> outscore the national chains by wide margins. The study confirms that supermarkets and mass merchandisers outperform chains on key metrics: sufficient staff, trust in pharmacists, ease of ordering, and fill speed. Chain drug store closures are creating opportunities for supermarkets, mass merchandisers, and online pharmacies. <strong>63% of mail-order customers are interested in digital pharmacies</strong>, and <strong>33% of all customers lack awareness of digital pharmacy brands</strong>. Source: JD Power, 2025. <a href="https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2025-us-pharmacy-study">jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2025-us-pharmacy-study</a></p></div>
  <div class="method-card"><span class="status completed">Completed</span><h4>U.S. Census Bureau — Prosper, TX</h4><p>Multiple Census sources provide the demographic foundation for StarCare's target market. The <strong>QuickFacts dataset</strong> reports a 2025 population estimate of <strong>45,605</strong> (up 51.2% since 2020, when it was 30,174), with a median household income of <strong>$195,281</strong> — one of the highest in Texas. <strong>32.7% of residents are under 18</strong>, <strong>66.5% hold a bachelor's degree or higher</strong>, and <strong>87.9% of housing is owner-occupied</strong>. The <strong>American Community Survey 5-year estimates</strong> (censusreporter.org) report <strong>37,869 residents</strong> across 11,239 households, with a median age of 37.6, per capita earnings of $72,601, and a poverty rate of just 5.5%. The <strong>City of Prosper's own statistics</strong> (prospertx.gov) estimate <strong>49,355 residents as of January 2026</strong>, covering 27 square miles, with a median family earnings of $177,244 and average property value of $866,650. Together, these sources confirm that Prosper is a young, affluent, highly-educated family community — ideal for a pharmacy focused on long-term patient relationships. Sources: <a href="https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/prospertowntexas">census.gov/quickfacts/prospertowntexas</a>, <a href="https://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US4859696-prosper-tx/">censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US4859696-prosper-tx</a>, <a href="https://www.prospertx.gov/302/Stats-Demographics">prospertx.gov/302/Stats-Demographics</a></p></div>
  <div class="method-card"><span class="status completed">Completed</span><h4>IQVIA — Launch Strategy & Commercialization Research</h4><p>IQVIA's analysis of over <strong>1,000 innovative launches across three decades</strong> provides the strategic framework for Project Swastik's implementation timeline.   <h3>Rationale</h3>
  <p>These objectives are not arbitrary. They are based on benchmarks from Apollo Pharmacy's CRM system, which achieved similar improvements in a comparable independent pharmacy setting. The 42% to 78% return rate target is ambitious but achievable — Apollo went from 28% to 42% in their first six months with a basic CRM system. StarCare has an advantage Apollo didn't: a pre-existing culture of personal relationships. The chains score lowest in customer satisfaction (643/1,000 for CVS) because they lack personal relationships. StarCare has those relationships — the strategy is to make them measurable and consistent.</p>
  <h2>Pillar 1: Drishti - Transparent Care</h2>
  <p><strong>The Problem:</strong> Billing and insurance confusion is the #1 driver of patient churn at StarCare. The gap between the 4.5-star Google rating and 2.6-star Yelp rating is entirely billing-related. This is the single biggest leak in StarCare's retention bucket.</p>
  <p><strong>What We'll Do:</strong></p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Pre-fill copay estimates</strong> — Before every prescription is filled, the patient gets a clear written estimate of their copay. No surprises at pickup. This directly addresses the #1 Yelp complaint.</li>
    <li><strong>Prior authorization alerts</strong> — Automated alerts sent 3-5 days before the PA deadline, giving patients time to respond to their prescriber and avoid fill delays.</li>
    <li><strong>Post-visit SMS surveys</strong> — Within 24 hours of prescription pickup, patients receive a 3-question SMS survey: copay clarity, wait time satisfaction, and overall rating. Negative responses trigger a follow-up call within 4 hours.</li>
  </ol>
  <div class="key-insight"><strong>WHY THIS WORKS</strong>The pre-fill copay estimate directly addresses the #1 Yelp complaint. The PA alert reduces fill delays. The post-visit survey catches issues before they become negative reviews. Together, these three initiatives transform StarCare's weakest area into a trust-building opportunity.</div>
