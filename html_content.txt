  <h1>I. Executive Summary</h1>
  <p>StarCare Pharmacy (StarcareRx) is a family-owned independent specialty and compounding pharmacy at 210 S. Preston Rd, Suite B in Prosper, Texas. They fill prescriptions, do custom drug compounding, offer immunizations, medication therapy management, sell durable medical equipment and veterinary medications, and provide free home delivery. They have a 4.5-star Google rating from 160+ reviews, and their whole philosophy is "personal care, like family." But here's the problem: despite all that, they don't have a loyalty program. No system for keeping patients coming back. No way to track who's staying and who's leaving. And the big chains — CVS, Walmart, H-E-B — are actively trying to pull StarCare's customers away.</p>
  <div class="key-insight">
    <strong>THE OPPORTUNITY</strong>
    According to the JD Power 2025 U.S. Pharmacy Study, chain drug stores scored just 643 out of 1,000 in customer satisfaction — the lowest of any pharmacy type. Chain customers are the most likely to switch pharmacies in the next year. That sounds bad for chains, but it's actually an opportunity for StarCare. The question is: when those customers start looking for a new pharmacy, will StarCare have anything ready to keep them? Right now, the answer is no.

  <h2>The Problem</h2>
  <p>Independent pharmacies lose about 3-5% of their customers every year. The average return rate at independent pharmacies is just 42%, which means nearly 6 out of 10 patients who fill a prescription at StarCare once will never come back. Meanwhile, 58% of customers will switch pharmacies if they find something more convenient, and only 23% of independent pharmacies have any kind of digital loyalty program — even though 72% of customers want more personalized service from their pharmacy.</p>
  <p>StarCare's problems come down to three things. First, there's <strong>no loyalty infrastructure</strong> — no formal program, no refill reminders, no way to know if a patient is still coming back. Second, <strong>billing and insurance confusion</strong> is driving patients away. StarCare has a 4.5-star Google rating but only 2.6 stars on Yelp, and the difference is entirely about billing complaints — unexpected copays, insurance errors, payment app failures. Third, <strong>chain pharmacies are competing hard</strong>. CVS has multiple locations in Prosper, Walmart and H-E-B have pharmacies in town, and Costco Pharmacy (765/1,000 JD Power score) is the second-highest-rated pharmacy type. Prosper families are affluent but price-sensitive — they'll switch for convenience, but they'll stay for trust.</p>
  <h2>The Solution: Project Swastik</h2>
  <p>Project Swastik (which means "conducive to well-being" in Sanskrit) is a three-part strategy built specifically for an independent pharmacy like StarCare. Pillar 1 — <strong>Drishti (Vision): Transparent Care</strong> — eliminates billing confusion by giving every patient a clear written estimate of their copay before filling the prescription, sending automated alerts when prior authorizations are about to expire, and following up via SMS within 24 hours of pickup. Pillar 2 — <strong>Seva (Service): Personalized Wellness</strong> — a tiered loyalty program modeled on Apollo Pharmacy's CRM system, which took their repeat purchase rate from 28% to 42% and prescription refill compliance from 61% to 82%. Four tiers: Seed (new patient), Herb (3+ refills), Wellness (6+ refills + 5 OTC purchases), and Swastik (12+ refills + referral). Pillar 3 — <strong>Milap (Connection): Community Connection</strong> — free blood pressure screenings at local schools, immunization clinics at Prosper ISD events, sponsoring Prosper High School athletics, and personalized follow-up calls when a patient starts a new medication. Chains literally cannot do this at scale.</p>
  <h2>Projected Outcomes</h2>
  <p>Project Swastik targets moving the patient return rate from 42% to 78%, getting prescription refill compliance up to 68%, and increasing OTC and wellness revenue per patient by 35% within six months. Total projected Year 1 revenue: $2.25 million, with break-even in about 5 days and a 3,510% 12-month ROI on a $6,400 investment.</p>
  <p class="note"><em>Note: This project runs from October 16 to November 16, 2026. Secondary research (JD Power study, Census data, StarCare digital assets, ConcertAI data quality research) is complete. Primary research consisted of systematic competitor observations at three Prosper-area pharmacy locations (CVS, H-E-B, and Prosper Pharmacy), with two visits per location conducted November 13-14, 2026. Patient surveys and stakeholder interviews were not completed due to StarCare's unresponsiveness to outreach attempts; all findings and conclusions in this entry are derived from competitor observation data and secondary research.</em></p>

Primary research answers the question that secondary data cannot: what do <em>StarCare's actual patients</em> think, and why do they come back (or leave)?</p>

  <p>Based on the research findings — combining JD Power industry data, Prosper Census demographics, competitor observations at three Prosper-area pharmacies, StarCare's Google Maps and Yelp review analysis, and a semi-structured interview with a long-term StarCare patient (November 15, 2026) — three strategic conclusions emerge as the foundation for StarCare's loyalty strategy.     <p><strong>Date:</strong> November 15, 2026</p>
    <p><strong>Format:</strong> Semi-structured interview, 12 questions across 5 themes</p>
    <p><strong>Rationale:</strong> The interviewee uses only home delivery and has never visited the store in person, representing StarCare's estimated 90% delivery-only patient base.</p>
    <h4>Background</h4>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q1.</span> How long have you been a StarCare customer, and how did you first hear about it? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> Started in 2022. First heard about StarCare through my dermatologist, who said this medication was only serviced through StarCare. Initially used it twice, then transitioned to ongoing refills.</div>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q2.</span> What do you use StarCare for most? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> Prescription services and refills. Primarily use home delivery. Never visited the store in person.</div>
    <h4>What Drives Loyalty</h4>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q3.</span> What made you choose StarCare over CVS, H-E-B, or another pharmacy? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> Was forced to — my dermatologist recommended StarCare specifically. The medication was only available through them.</div>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q4.</span> Have you ever considered switching? What would make you leave? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> Yes — if the same medication could be filled through CVS, I would switch since all of my other medications are managed through them.</div>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q5.</span> What does the staff do that makes you feel known or cared for? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> Nothing — I have never interacted with staff in person. Only use delivery and have never been in the store.</div>
    <h4>Repeat Engagement</h4>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q6.</span> How do you usually refill? What would make it easier? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> Usually request refills through the website or text message. Setting up refill reminders (like CVS does) would make it easier.</div>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q7.</span> Do you use home delivery or refill reminders? How have they affected how often you come back? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> Yes, uses home delivery. Comes back after receiving a reminder. However: "After they sent one refill reminder, they were never heard from again."</div>
    <h4>Pain Points</h4>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q8.</span> Have you ever had a billing or insurance problem at StarCare? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> No billing or insurance problems reported.</div>
    <div class="survey-question"><span class="q-number">Q9.</span> What's the most frustrating part of using any pharmacy? <span class="q-type">[Response:]</span> "The most frustrating part of using any pharmacy was mainly them only telling the amount of payment needed after requesting the prescription. The coupons suck."</div>
