# Merchandise Planning & Buying

## Overview

Merchandise planning is the systematic process of deciding **what products to carry, in what quantities, and when to replenish them** — to hit revenue targets while controlling expenses.

## Planning Workflow

1. **Establish revenue and profit objectives** — set targets for the planning period
2. **Distribute targets across product categories** — allocate sales goals by line
3. **Factor in current stock levels and expected price reductions** — account for markdowns
4. **Apply the OTB equation** — determine purchasing limits
5. **Set reorder triggers and buffer stock thresholds** — prevent stockouts
6. **Continuously compare actual sales to forecasts** — adjust plans

## Assortment Planning

### Product Breadth vs. Depth

| Concept | Definition | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Breadth** | Number of different product lines carried | A store carries shirts, pants, shoes, accessories = 4 lines |
| **Depth** | Number of SKUs within a single product line | 5 colors × 4 sizes = 20 SKUs in the shirt line |

### SKU (Stock-Keeping Unit)
A unique tracking code assigned to each distinct product variation (color, size, style).

### Merchandise Mix
The combination of product lines and brands a retailer offers to meet the needs of its target market.

### Micromerchandising
Tailoring a store's product mix to local tastes and demographics. Critical for local business partnerships.

## Inventory Management

### Key Inventory Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Safety Stock** | Reserve inventory held to absorb unexpected demand surges or shipping delays |
| **Lead Time** | Elapsed time between submitting a purchase order and receiving the goods |
| **Stockout** | When an item is unavailable for sale — lost sales + lost customer goodwill |
| **Shrinkage** | Loss of inventory due to theft, damage, administrative errors |
| **FIFO** | First-In, First-Out — oldest stock sold first to prevent obsolescence |
| **Floor Ready Merchandise** | Packaging, folding, hanging, ticketing so merchandise is ready for the selling floor |

### Inventory Metrics to Track

- **Inventory Turnover** — efficiency of stock management
- **Days Sales of Inventory** — how long stock sits before selling
- **Stock-to-Sales Ratio** — months of supply on hand
- **GMROI** — return on inventory investment
- **Forecast Accuracy** — how well predictions match actuals
- **Shrinkage Rate** — inventory loss as % of sales

## Open-to-Buy (OTB) Deep Dive

### What is OTB?
The dollar amount available to purchase new merchandise for a specific period without exceeding inventory goals.

### Two Main Formula Versions

**Version 1 — Retail Basis (Most Common in DECA)**
```
Planned Purchases = Planned Sales + Planned Markdowns + Ending Inventory - Beginning Inventory
OTB = Planned Purchases - Merchandise On Order
```

**Version 2 — Cost Basis**
```
OTB = (Planned Sales / Gross Margin %) - (Beginning Inventory + Planned Receipts - Planned Markdowns)
```

### OTB Planning Best Practices

| Practice | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| Update monthly for stable products, weekly during promotions | Prevents plan drift |
| Use retail OTB for category budgets, cost OTB for cash flow | Different purposes |
| Track receipts on order | Existing POs consume your OTB budget |
| Don't mix retail, cost, and unit values | Keeps calculations consistent |
| Validate projections against actuals | Prevents budget drift |

### Common OTB Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---------|-------------|
| Applying OTB to staple items instead of reorder points | Over-ordering on basics |
| Ignoring lead-time variability | Breaks monthly planning |
| Failing to adjust for shrinkage | Inflated OTB numbers |
| Relying strictly on projections | Budget drift from reality |

## Replenishment Strategy

### When to Reorder
```
Reorder Point = (Average Daily Usage × Lead Time) + Safety Stock
```

### Example
```
Avg Daily Usage:     10 units
Lead Time:            7 days
Safety Stock:         5 units

Reorder Point = (10 × 7) + 5 = 75 units

When inventory drops to 75 units → place reorder
```

### Order Quantity
```
Order Quantity = Reorder Point + Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) - Current Stock
```

## Merchandise Planning Checklist

- [ ] Revenue and profit objectives established
- [ ] Sales targets allocated by product category
- [ ] Current stock levels assessed
- [ ] Expected markdowns planned
- [ ] OTB calculations completed (retail and cost basis)
- [ ] Reorder points set for all SKUs
- [ ] Safety stock levels determined
- [ ] Lead times confirmed with vendors
- [ ] Forecast compared to actuals — variances documented
- [ ] Plan adjusted for seasonality and trends
