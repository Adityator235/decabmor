# Pricing Strategies

## Pricing Strategy Types

### 1. Cost-Plus Pricing
Adding a predetermined markup to the unit cost.
```
Price = Cost × (1 + Markup %)
```
Simple and predictable, but ignores customer perception and competitor pricing.

### 2. Value-Based Pricing
Setting price based on the **perceived benefit to the customer** rather than cost.
- Highest potential margins
- Requires deep customer understanding
- Best for differentiated/premium products

### 3. Price Skimming
Launching at a high price to capture early adopters, then lowering over time.
- Best for innovative/unique products
- Maximizes revenue from price-insensitive customers first
- Risk: attracts competitors quickly

### 4. Penetration Pricing
Setting a low initial price to gain market share quickly.
- Best for competitive markets
- Builds customer base fast
- Risk: hard to raise prices later, lower margins

### 5. Psychological Pricing
Pricing that exploits consumer perception.
- **Charm pricing**: $9.99 instead of $10.00
- **Prestige pricing**: Round numbers signal quality ($100, not $99.99)
- **Bundle pricing**: Multiple items at a perceived discount
- **Reference pricing**: Showing "was $X, now $Y"

## Markup vs. Markdown

### Markup
```
Markup $ = Selling Price - Cost
Markup % = (Markup $ / Cost) × 100
```

### Markdown
A downward adjustment in retail price, typically to:
- Stimulate sales of slow-moving inventory
- Clear seasonal merchandise
- Match competitor pricing
- Dispose of discontinued items

### Keystoning
Doubling an item's cost to establish a retail price (50% markup on cost, 50% gross margin).
```
Retail Price = Cost × 2
```
The most common pricing strategy in retail.

### Loss Leader
A product sold **below cost** to attract traffic for higher-margin items.
- Used strategically, not as a default
- Must drive sufficient traffic to offset the loss

## Initial Markup vs. Maintained Markup

### Initial Markup
The markup added to cost to establish the **first** retail price.
```
Initial Markup % = (Operating Expenses + Profit + Reductions + Alterations - Cash Discounts) / Gross Sales × 100
```

**Reductions include**: markdowns, employee discounts, shrinkage, returns

### Maintained (Achieved) Markup
The actual markup after all reductions have occurred.
```
Maintained Markup % = (Net Sales - Cost of Goods Sold) / Net Sales × 100
```

### Key Insight
```
Maintained Markup = Initial Markup - Reductions
```
If you plan for 40% initial markup but expect 10% in reductions, your maintained markup will be ~30%.

## Pricing Tactics for Loyalty Programs

### Tiered Pricing
- Higher tiers get better prices/discounts
- Incentivizes customers to spend more to reach next tier

### Member-Only Pricing
- Exclusive prices for loyalty program members
- Creates perceived value in joining the program

### Dynamic Pricing
- Prices change based on demand, time, or customer data
- **54% of shoppers dislike dynamic pricing** — use cautiously
- Can damage brand trust if perceived as unfair

### Behavioral Pricing
- Offer personalized discounts based on purchase history
- Reward repeat purchases with targeted offers
- Use purchase data to predict price sensitivity

## Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

```
PED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Price)
```

| PED Value | Type | Pricing Strategy |
|-----------|------|------------------|
| \|PED\| > 1 | Elastic | Price cuts increase revenue; price hikes decrease revenue |
| \|PED\| < 1 | Inelastic | Price hikes increase revenue; price cuts decrease revenue |
| \|PED\| = 1 | Unit elastic | Revenue unchanged by price changes |

### Application to Loyalty
- Loyal customers tend to have **more inelastic demand** — less price sensitive
- Goal: increase loyalty → decrease elasticity → protect margins

## Key Retail Pricing Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Initial Markup** | Markup added to cost to establish the first retail price |
| **Maintained Markup** | Difference between cost and actual selling price after reductions |
| **Keystoning** | Doubling an item's cost to establish retail price |
| **Loss Leader** | Product sold below cost to drive traffic |
| **Markdown** | Downward adjustment in retail price |
| **Price Elasticity** | How sensitive demand is to price changes |
| **Psychological Pricing** | Pricing that exploits consumer perception |
| **Value-Based Pricing** | Price set by perceived customer benefit |
| **Price Skimming** | High initial price, lowered over time |
| **Penetration Pricing** | Low initial price to gain market share |
| **Anticipation Discount** | Additional discount for paying before cash discount period |
| **Cumulative Discount** | Discount applying to orders placed over a period |
| **Quantity Discount** | Discount on cost based on amount ordered |
