III. Research Methods (continued)

B. Primary Research Plan (continued)

Phase 2: Structured Competitor Observations

Structured observations will be conducted at the four primary competitor pharmacies in Prosper. Each pharmacy will be visited twice: once during a weekday afternoon (Tuesday, 2:00–4:00 PM) and once during a Saturday morning (Saturday, 10:00 AM–12:00 PM) to capture different shopping patterns.

Observation Dimension What Is Recorded Why It Matters
Store layout and traffic flow Entrance path, high-visibility zones, checkout placement Identifies merchandising best practices and customer flow patterns
Loyalty program visibility Signage at entrance, checkout, and throughout store Measures how actively each pharmacy promotes its loyalty program
OTC/wellness merchandising Product placement, display quality, seasonal promotions Assesses how competitors drive OTC sales (28% of StarCare revenue)
Customer behavior Average basket size, dwell time in sections, interaction with pharmacist Qualitative assessment of shopping habits and loyalty signals
Staff-customer interaction Friendly greetings, issue resolution, pharmacist availability Service quality is a key loyalty driver; observed directly
Pricing transparency Visible price tags, copay communication, price match signage Measures how each pharmacy communicates pricing — a key StarCare improvement area

Phase 3: Stakeholder Interviews

Semi-structured interviews will be conducted with 8–10 stakeholders in the Prosper pharmacy market. Interviewees will include:

Stakeholder Group Target n Interview Focus
StarCare patients 4 What makes them loyal to StarCare? What would make them switch? How do they learn about pharmacy changes?
Competitor pharmacy patients 2 What drives their pharmacy choice? How do they compare StarCare to their current pharmacy?
StarCare staff/pharmacists 2 What do they observe about patient preferences, peak times, and loyalty signals? What retention strategies have they seen work?
Local business owners (Prosper) 2 What loyalty strategies work in local retail? What have they seen succeed or fail in the Prosper market?

Each interview will last 20–30 minutes and will be conducted in person or via video call. Interviews will be guided by a semi-structured protocol with 8–10 open-ended questions, allowing follow-up probes based on individual responses.

C. Secondary Research

Secondary research will draw on peer-reviewed academic journals, industry reports, government demographic data, and published case studies to contextualize primary findings. Key sources include:

Source Category Examples Purpose
Academic journals Journal of Pharmacy Practice, JAMA Health Forum Theoretical framework for loyalty drivers in independent pharmacy
Industry reports JD Power U.S. Pharmacy Study, NCPA/America's Pharmacist, LeapFrog Investments Market trends in pharmacy loyalty and retention
Government data U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey Demographic trends for Prosper and Collin County
Case studies Apollo Pharmacy (CRM), Goodlife Pharmacy (loyalty redesign) Real-world examples of successful pharmacy loyalty strategies

D. Limitations

This study has three limitations. First, the survey sample (n = 150) is not a probability sample — it is a convenience sample distributed through community channels. Results are descriptive, not inferential; they indicate direction and magnitude but should not be generalized to all Prosper residents. Second, competitor observations are conducted by a single observer and are subject to observer bias. Third, self-reported survey data may be subject to response bias (e.g., overreporting loyalty to a preferred pharmacy).

These limitations will be mitigated through triangulation: findings from surveys, observations, and interviews will be cross-referenced to identify convergent patterns. Where primary data is limited, secondary sources will be used to validate or challenge initial conclusions.

ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS All survey participants will provide informed consent. Responses will be anonymized and used solely for academic purposes. Competitor observations will be conducted in public areas of each pharmacy without recording private information. Interview participants will be informed that their responses may be cited in the final entry but will not be attributed by name. No patient health information (PHI) will be collected or recorded, in compliance with HIPAA regulations.
