## 1. Industry Overview

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market size (2025) | $1.74 billion |
| Projected market size (2034) | $3.84 billion |
| Growth rate | 9.2% CAGR |
| Locations globally | 3,450+ cafes |
| Hobby games channel (2025) | $3.66B (grew 40% despite tariffs) |
| NAICS 451120 sectoral output | $25.19B (2025), up from $22.03B (2024) |

**Sources:** ICv2 Hobby Games White Paper (GAMA Expo 2026), FRED/BLS, marketintelo.com, BoardGameGeek analysis

### Key Industry Trend

53% of consumers are actively trying to reduce phone time — up 20 points in the last two years. This is the single biggest driver of board game cafe demand. Gen Z is the most screen-fatigued generation ever, with 2/3 preferring in-store shopping.

The hobby games channel grew 17 consecutive years through 2025, reaching $3.66B. Trading card games (TCGs) dominated with ~$1B growth, now accounting for ~2/3 of hobby games dollars. Board games were flat/down, but card games and RPGs (D&D 2024 remains the biggest recruitment driver) are thriving.

Tariffs (import tax hikes starting April 2025) and the Diamond Comic Distributors bankruptcy were major disruptions — yet the channel still grew. Publishers are using Kickstarter/Gamefound to cut tariff risk. Gamefound reported $85M+ in tabletop pledges in 2024.

## 2. Competitive Landscape — DFW/Frisco Area

### Direct Competitors

| Competitor | Location | Concept | Pricing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nerdvana** | 5757 Main St, Frisco | Upscale gaming restaurant + craft bar, board + video games | Premium pricing, no reservations, first-come first-served |
| **Texas Tabletop Society** | 8050 Preston Rd, Frisco | Dedicated board game library, 1,000+ games | $11/hr/person or $22 flat; $22/table + $6/player for groups |
| **Farm+Feed** | Shops at Legacy North, Plano | Gaming restaurant, console + PC + board games | $5/hr booths, full restaurant menu |

### Indirect Competitors

| Competitor | Location | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Common Ground Games** | Dallas | Anchor tabletop store (shop, not cafe) |
| **Dallas Games Marathon** | Plano | Nonprofit-style game center, large public library |
| **Warhammer Store** | Frisco | Miniatures/wargaming specialty |
| **Mason's Cards and Collectibles** | Frisco | TCGs and collectibles, 4.5★ rating |

### Competitive Gap Analysis

The DFW area leans heavily toward **hybrid gaming restaurants** (Nerdvana, Farm+Feed) and **large hobby stores** — not classic board game cafes. Key gaps:

1. **No daytime family focus** — Texas Tabletop Society operates 6-10pm weekdays, no Sunday access for non-members. Nerdvana opens at 4pm weekdays. No competitor serves families well before 5pm.
2. **No retail + experience hybrid** — Competitors are either pure experience (Texas Tabletop) or pure retail (Common Ground Games). None combine "try before you buy" with F&B.
3. **Limited accessibility** — Texas Tabletop is members-only for Sundays, evening-only hours. Nerdvana takes no reservations.
4. **No UT Dallas integration** — 15 minutes from UT Dallas (38,000+ students), no competitor targets this massive Gen Z demographic intentionally.

### Strategic Positioning

Our board game cafe fills the gap between **experience entertainment** and **retail** — a daytime-accessible space with strong F&B, family hours, a "try before you buy" retail component, and intentional programming for UT Dallas students, Frisco families, and young professionals.

## 3. Customer Demographics & Behavior

### Primary Segments

| Segment | Age | % of Traffic | Spend/Visit | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gen Z | 13-19 | ~40% of volume | $18-28 | 12-24x/year (enthusiasts) |
| Millennials | 20-35 | ~45% of volume | $35-48 | 8-12x/year (core) |
| Gen X / Older | 36-50 | ~15% of volume | $40-55 | 4-6x/year |

**Average spend per visit:** $32-45 (combining game fees, F&B, retail)

### Key Demographic Insights

- **Millennials (25-45)** are the revenue drivers — highest spend ($35-48/visit), consistent frequency (8-12x/year)
- **Gen Z** drives volume and word-of-mouth — lower spend but higher frequency (enthusiasts visit 12-24x/year)
- A Jakarta study found 89% of visitors were aged 23-27, with 6% aged 17-22 (74% male skew, but varies by region)
- A Thai study found customers visit more than once every two weeks, often staying 3-4 hours in the evening
- One study found 91% of customers visited 3-5 times per month

### Primary Motivations

1. **Social connection** — desire to disconnect from screens and engage in genuine face-to-face interaction
2. **Community** — venues serve as "third places" for socializing, dating, corporate team-building
3. **Novelty and variety** — 300-800 curated games ranging from classic nostalgia to modern strategy (Catan, Ticket to Ride)
4. **Events** — birthday parties, game nights, tournaments, designer demos

### Spending Patterns

- Food & Beverage: 48-62% of total revenue
- Game Rentals & Sales: 22-35% of total revenue
- Millennials willing to pay premium for craft cocktails, coffee, and food
- Gen Z drawn to variety of games and social experience over material goods

## 5. F&B Operations — The Profit Engine

### Core Principle

> Build a restaurant that lends games, not a game library that serves food.

The F&B operation is the profit engine that pays the rent. Cover charges alone cannot sustain the business.

### Menu Strategy

- **12-20 items**, executed consistently — avoid menu bloat
- Design for **repeat ordering** across a long visit (drink + snack → shareable plate → dessert)
- Food must be **finger-friendly** — flatbreads, pretzels, charcuterie, bowls; avoid saucy or greasy items
- Target kitchen ticket time: **under 12 minutes**
- Re-run recipe costs quarterly; cut bottom 2-3 sellers each quarter

### Margin Breakdown by Category

| Item | Food Cost | Gross Margin |
|---|---|---|
| Non-alcoholic beverages (coffee, craft soda) | 15-25% | **75-85%** |
| Beer & wine | 22-30% | **70-78%** |
| Food (blended) | 28-35% | **65-70%** |
| Retail games | 50-70% | **30-50%** |

### Key F&B KPIs

| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| Spend per guest | $18-28 |
| Table turns | 1.0-1.5 on weekends |
| RevPASH (Revenue Per Available Seat Hour) | $8-12 at peak |
| Food waste/shrinkage | 4-5% |
| Prime cost (COGS + labor) | 58-65% |

### Beverage Strategy

- Beverages are the **highest-margin** items — drive them hard
- Beer/wine programs lift check average meaningfully; beer-and-wine licenses are easier to obtain than full liquor
- Consider a full bar if market and licensing allow (highest margin lift)
- Non-alcoholic: craft sodas, specialty coffee, tea — 75-85% margin

## 6. Loyalty & Retention Strategies

### Tiered Membership Model

| Tier | Visits/Month | Perks |
|---|---|---|
| **Explorer** | 5 | 1-week early access to new games |
| **Strategist** | 10 | 2-week early access + voting rights on new game purchases |
| **Master** | 15+ | 3-week early access + exclusive preview nights + reservation priority |

### Retention Tactics

1. **Points-based system** — earn on game fees, F&B purchases, retail. Redeem for free table time, complimentary drinks, early access to new games. First-visit bonus (e.g., 150 points) incentivizes return visits.

2. **Push notifications via Apple/Google Wallet** — ~90% open rate vs ~20% for email newsletters. Timed notifications (e.g., 3pm on game nights) drive immediate action.

3. **New acquisition voting** — loyal members vote on which games to add to the library. Creates ownership and investment in the space.

4. **Weekly "New Player Hour"** — onboarding facilitators help first-timers learn games. Removes barrier to entry.

5. **Monthly competitive ladders** — leaderboards for popular games (Catan, Ticket to Ride, 7 Wonders). Creates recurring competitive motivation.

6. **Win-back messaging** — automated outreach at 30/60/90-day intervals for lapsed visitors. Personalized with "games you haven't played in a while."

7. **Designer demo nights & publisher launches** — exclusive events for tier members. Creates FOMO and community.

8. **Digital stamp collections & seasonal campaigns** — gamification within the loyalty program.

### Retention Metrics to Track

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| Repeat attendance rate | 30/60/90-day return rates |
| First-timer-to-member conversion | % of first visits that convert to paid members |
| Revenue per seat-hour | Total revenue / available seat-hours |
| Net Promoter Score (NPS) | Customer loyalty indicator |
| Average revenue per user (ARPU) | Total revenue / total unique customers |
| Volunteer retention | % of community event volunteers retained month-over-month |

### Programming Strategy

- **60% recurring community nights** — weekly game nights, tournaments, new player hours
- **40% special events** — birthday parties, designer demos, publisher launches, themed nights
- Inclusive programming for families, seniors, and neurodiverse groups

## 7. Merchandise Buying & Inventory Management

### Game Library Sizing

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Total games (2,500 sqft) | 300-800 curated titles |
| Library game depreciation | 3-5 years (capital cost, not COGS) |
| Replace unused games | After 6 months of zero plays |
| Annual library refresh | 15-20% of titles |
| Initial library cost | $8,000-20,000 |

### Library Composition

| Category | % of Library | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Party games | 30% | Highest turnover, drives volume |
| Eurogames/Strategy | 30% | Core audience, higher spend |
| Family games | 20% | Weekend family traffic |
| TCGs (Pokémon, MTG) | 10% | Recurring weekly traffic |
| Cooperative | 10% | Couples, casual players |

### Buying Channels

| Channel | MOQ | Terms | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Faire** | 1 unit | Net-60, free returns on first order | Testing new titles, low risk |
| **Asmodee** | Varies by tier | MAP-compliant pricing | Well-known titles, demo copies |
| **Meeples Distribution** | Automated | €25/mo subscription | POS-integrated auto-reordering |

### Asmodee Account Tiers

| Tier | Requirements | Benefits |
|---|---|---|
| **Flagship** | Brick-and-mortar only | Early access, organized play eligibility, demo copies |
| **Toy & Gift** | Brick-and-mortar required | No organized play |
| **General** | No brick-and-mortar requirement | Eligible for organized play |

**MAP Policy:** Asmodee enforces Minimum Advertised Pricing across all channels; products restricted from Amazon.

### Retail Pricing Strategy

- Games marked up **40-55%** off wholesale
- MAP policies prevent discounting top publishers' products
- "Try before you buy" — games on display, customers can play then purchase at full retail
- Demo copies available for eligible publisher accounts (Flagship tier)

### Inventory Management Best Practices

- **Automated reordering:** Meeples POS analyzes in-store sales data to recommend restocking (automates 95% of purchasing)
- **Sales tracking:** Track which games sell most/least; rotate out bottom performers quarterly
- **Trend monitoring:** Kickstarter/Gamefound for emerging titles before they hit mainstream
- **Seasonal rotation:** Holiday-themed games in Q4, family games in summer, TCG tournaments year-round

## 9. Alignment with DECA BMOR 2026-27 Topic

**Competition theme:** "Rebuilding Customer Loyalty and Spending"

Board game cafe is an ideal fit because:

1. **The industry IS a loyalty/retention business** — repeat visits are the entire model, not an afterthought
2. **Loyalty programs built around community, not transactions** — voting on new games, tiered access, exclusive events
3. **Customer spending measured across 3 streams** — game fees, F&B, retail (multiple revenue levers to analyze)
4. **Retention strategies are multi-layered** — points, community, events, exclusivity, gamification
5. **Data is abundant and specific** — RevPASH, ARPU, 30/60/90-day return rates, NPS, conversion funnels
