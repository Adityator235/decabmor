The Problem

Board gamers in North America spend an average of $497 per year on hobbies (Statista, 2025), yet the primary entertainment options in Frisco are designed for screen-based activities. Existing board game cafes — including Nerdvana, the area's only competitor — operate exclusively during evening and weekend hours, effectively abandoning the 10 AM to 4 PM daytime window where families, remote workers, and college students are available to visit.

Additionally, the rising cost of new board games — average $54 per game with premium titles exceeding $100 — creates a barrier to entry for the core demographic of teenagers and young adults. The pre-owned market exists but is fragmented across eBay, Facebook Marketplace, and local game stores with no quality guarantee or return policy.

The Solution

The Game Table solves both problems through a retail + experience hybrid model:

Daytime access. By opening at 10 AM on weekdays — two hours before the typical board game cafe — The Game Table captures a temporal gap that no local competitor is exploiting. This window serves parents with young children, UT Dallas students between classes, and remote workers seeking a daytime social activity.

Pre-owned marketplace. A curated buy-back and resale program lets customers trade games at fair prices, reducing their cost of participation while generating 40–55% gross margins on pre-owned sales for The Game Table.

Turn the P.A.G.E. loyalty campaign. A four-pillar strategy (Pre-Owned Marketplace, App-Based Transparency, Giving & Awareness, Employee Engagement) rebuilds customer loyalty through community engagement rather than transactional discounts — directly addressing the research finding that 68% of customers cite "community feel" as their top loyalty driver (Loyalty Pass, 2025).

II. Introduction

A. Description of the Business

The Game Table is a board game cafe and retail store planned for Frisco, Texas. The business model operates on three revenue streams: a cover charge that grants customers access to a library of 300 to 800 board games, food and beverage sales, and retail game merchandise. The facility will occupy approximately 2,500 square feet, featuring communal tables for group play, intimate seating areas for couples and small parties, and retail shelving displaying games for purchase.

The food and beverage program is designed to complement the gaming experience. The menu will feature 12 to 20 items specifically selected for their compatibility with gameplay — flatbreads, pretzels, charcuterie boards, and grain bowls. Items that are excessively saucy or greasy have been excluded, as they interfere with the ability to handle cards and game pieces cleanly. The beverage program will include specialty coffee, craft sodas, tea service, and beer and wine where licensing permits.

The purpose of The Game Table extends beyond traditional retail. It functions as a "third place" — a social surrounding separate from the two usual social environments of home and the workplace (Oldenburg, 1999). Customers come to play games, meet new people, celebrate birthdays, and engage in face-to-face interaction. The business generates revenue through food sales and game purchases, but its competitive advantage lies in its role as a community gathering space. 🎲

Each visit unit generates revenue from multiple cover charges and higher F&B orders than a single customer.

This initial loyalty strategy serves as the foundation upon which the "Turn the P.A.G.E." campaign will be built. The full strategic plan, detailed in Section V of this entry, expands this basic points system into a comprehensive four-pillar approach encompassing pre-owned game sales, app-based engagement, community giving, and employee engagement.

Pillar 1: Pre-Owned Marketplace

P Reduce the Cost of Participation

The Game Table will operate a pre-owned game marketplace where customers can buy, sell, and trade board games at fair prices. The average board game costs $54 new, with premium titles exceeding $100 — a significant barrier for the student and young-family demographics that form our core market (Newzoo, 2025).

How it works: Customers bring in gently used games for evaluation. The Game Table purchases them at 30–40% of retail price and resells them at 50–60% of retail, generating a 40–55% gross margin. Games are displayed in a dedicated "Pre-Owned Corner" with clear condition grading (Mint, Excellent, Good, Fair).

Buy-back rules: To prevent arbitrage (customers buying full-price games and immediately selling them back for store credit), buy-back credit is capped at $25 per month per member and requires participation in at least one community activity per quarter (attending a community night, hosting a game tutorial, or volunteering at an event). This ensures rewards go to genuinely engaged members, not opportunistic resellers.

Loyalty connection: Trade Nights — monthly events where customers can bring games to swap or purchase — create recurring foot traffic during the daytime window when no competitor is active. Pre-owned purchases also earn loyalty points at the same rate as new games, removing friction from the buy-back loop.

Pillar 2: App-Based Transparency

A Build Trust Through Open Communication

The Game Table will launch a simple mobile-friendly web app that provides customers with real-time information: current game availability, wait times, upcoming event schedules, and their loyalty point balance. Research shows that 45% of successful board game cafes already use a mobile app or digital platform to enhance the customer experience (VoxBooster, 2026).

Features:

• Game Search: Customers can search our catalog of 300–800 games and see real-time availability
• Event Calendar: Upcoming game nights, tournaments, and community events with RSVP
• Loyalty Dashboard: Track points, rewards, and pre-owned game transactions
• Feedback Loop: One-tap rating system so customers can share their experience instantly

Loyalty connection: Transparency builds trust. When customers know exactly what to expect — wait times, game availability, pricing — they feel more confident returning. The app also serves as a digital touchpoint between visits, keeping The Game Table top-of-mind. — End of Page 12 — — Start of Page 13 —

Pillar 4: Employee Engagement

E Our Team Is Our First Customers

Research consistently shows that the single most important factor driving customer loyalty is staff who know customers by name and genuinely care about their experience (Loyalty Pass, 2025; Business Plan Templates, 2025). Employee engagement is the foundation of everything else.

Initiatives:

• Game Master Certification: Every employee completes a training program where they learn to teach 20+ games, ensuring every customer has a great experience
• Employee Choice Nights: Each staff member picks one game night per month to design and host — building ownership and fresh energy
• Customer Name Program: Employees are encouraged (not required) to learn regular customers' names and game preferences; recognition awards given monthly
• Student Worker Program: Partner with UT Dallas to hire student workers who bring energy and board game expertise

Super-connector recognition: Beyond transactional rewards (points for spending), The Game Table will recognize members who create social value — the person who brings five friends to a game night, the regular who teaches newcomers how to play, the parent who volunteers at a community event. These "super-connectors" receive exclusive perks: early access to new games, invitations to private preview events, and a named spot on a "Community Builder" wall in-store. This leverages the psychological principle that intrinsic social recognition is a stronger loyalty driver than extrinsic discounts (Deci & Ryan, Self-Determination Theory, 2000).

Loyalty connection: When employees are engaged, customers feel it. A smiling Game Master who remembers your favorite game type creates an experience no discount code can replicate.
